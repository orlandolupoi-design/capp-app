# Pilot Kit: Manufacturer Reimbursement Recovery (ACL s274)

Goal of the pilot: 3 caravan/RV dealers, 20 claims lodged by hand, measure how many get paid within 60 days.
Get a consumer-law lawyer to review this kit before you use it. This is not legal advice.

---

## 1. Dealer pitch (phone or in person, under 60 seconds)

> "When you fix, replace or refund a faulty van for a customer, the Australian Consumer Law says the manufacturer has to pay you back if the fault was theirs. That's section 274. Most dealers just wear the cost. The ACCC found 40% of caravan dealers have had a manufacturer refuse to reimburse them.
>
> We go through the last three years of repair and goodwill jobs you've paid for, work out which ones the manufacturer legally owes you, and claim them for you. No upfront cost. We take 25% of whatever we recover, and nothing if we recover nothing.
>
> Can I do a free audit of your last 12 months of job cards to show you the number?"

**Common objections**
- *"I don't want to upset the manufacturer."* Claims are made under a legal right that can't be contracted out of (s276). The government's Nov 2025 impact statement recommends penalties for manufacturers that retaliate. Start with the brands you're least dependent on.
- *"We already claim warranty."* This covers what warranty doesn't: goodwill repairs, out-of-warranty faults, diagnosis time, loan vans, towing, refunds.
- *"Too much paperwork."* We do the paperwork. You give us read access to job cards and invoices.

## 2. Audit checklist (per job card)

Flag a job as **claimable** only if all of these are true:

- [ ] The dealer supplied the goods to a consumer (ACL consumer definition met)
- [ ] The dealer provided a remedy (repair, replacement, refund or compensation) at its own cost
- [ ] The fault plausibly breaches a consumer guarantee (usually acceptable quality, s54), considering:
  - [ ] age of the van at the time of the fault
  - [ ] kilometres / usage
  - [ ] price paid
  - [ ] nature of the defect (manufacturing fault, not misuse, accident or dealer workmanship)
- [ ] The manufacturer is identifiable (including the importer, for imported vans)
- [ ] The remedy was provided **less than 3 years ago** (s274(4) limit; confirm start date with lawyer)
- [ ] The cost was **not** already reimbursed under the manufacturer warranty

**Evidence to collect for each claimable job**
- Job card with technician notes and date
- Photos of the defect
- Customer complaint (email, form or note)
- Invoices: parts, labour hours and rate, sublet work
- Extra costs: diagnosis time, loan or replacement van, towing, transport
- Proof of payment / write-off in the dealer's books
- Vehicle details: VIN, build date, sale date, sale price, km or usage

**Audit output for the dealer**
- Total jobs reviewed
- Jobs flagged claimable
- Total claimable value, by manufacturer
- Jobs close to the 3-year limit (claim these first)

**Pilot success test (after 60 days)**
- About half of claims paid, in full or part → the business works. Move to 10 dealers.
- Most refused → note the reasons by manufacturer, test the lawyer-letter step on 5 refusals.
- Dealers won't sign up → the fear-of-retaliation problem is bigger than expected; wait for the reform or switch to multi-brand appliance retailers.

---

## 5. Before you start
- Register an ABN and a business name
- Get professional indemnity insurance
- Have a consumer-law lawyer review the pitch, checklist and letter, and confirm the s274(4) 3-year limit start date
- Sign a simple services agreement with each dealer (contingency fee, data access, confidentiality)
- Find a partner law firm for refusals (fees charged separately; don't share legal fees)
